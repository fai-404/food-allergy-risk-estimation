Did You Know?
Important facts about food allergies
Food allergy involves the immune system reacting to a protein, while food intolerance is a digestive issue — completely different conditions.
Milk allergy is one of the most common in children, but around 80% of children outgrow it by age 16.
Cross-contamination in shared kitchens is a major hidden risk — even tiny traces of an allergen can trigger a severe reaction.
South Indian dishes often contain coconut, mustard seeds, and lentils — common hidden allergens many people overlook.
Ghee is clarified butter and still contains milk proteins — people with dairy allergy must avoid it even in small amounts.

Intolerance vs Allergy
Know the difference
Food Allergy: Immune system reacts to a food protein. Symptoms appear within minutes — hives, swelling, anaphylaxis. Even tiny amounts can trigger it.
Food Intolerance: Digestive system can't properly process a food. Symptoms are slower — bloating, cramps, diarrhea. Usually dose-dependent.
Lactose Intolerance is NOT the same as milk allergy. Lactose-intolerant people can often tolerate small amounts of dairy unlike those with milk allergy.
Celiac Disease is an autoimmune reaction to gluten — different from gluten sensitivity, which is milder and doesn't damage the intestine lining.

Allergy News & Updates
Recent research and tips
Research
Early exposure may reduce peanut allergy risk in infants
Studies show introducing peanut-containing foods early (4–6 months) significantly lowers the risk of developing peanut allergy later in life.
Tip
Always check restaurant menus for allergen disclosures
Many Indian restaurants now list the 14 major allergens on menus — always ask staff if you don't see it displayed clearly.
Warning
Sesame added as 9th major allergen in the US (2023)
Sesame seeds and sesame oil must now be declared on all packaged food labels in the United States under FASTER Act.
Update
AI food recognition tools being integrated into allergy apps
Multiple health apps are now using MobileNet-based models similar to AllerScan to help users identify allergens from food photos in real time.
